## Measured remaining allocator constraints

Alpha's first mismatch is primaryMatched's initial zero: v37 colors r0,
graph degree 8 and priority 165, but retail uses r4. Its neighboring v38
colors r4, v39 r3 and v40 r0; retail wants r3, r4 and r6 for those zeros.
These virtual slots are reused for values in later blocks, including the
candidate-byte comparison. Changing dictionary range tests to direct switches
reduces named index weight 208 to 199 without moving r29. The temporary graph
changes from 171 priority nodes/55 coalesces to 174/53 but leaves v37-v40's
colors unchanged. Direct predicate-call variants were also tested at one,
three and six sites; none is exact and all lose data.

Prepare's structural candidate keeps v62, v103 and v104 as call-result copies
where retail uses r3 directly. v62 has degree 7 and priority 189, selecting
r4; v103 has degree 6 and priority 150, selecting r0; v104 has degree 7 and
priority 149, selecting r4. Each interferes with r3 in another lifetime of
that same virtual slot. v62 also addresses strokePrefix in B117; v103/v104
form the index/address for a record-mask store in B28. Its 255 literal shares
a virtual slot with a result coalesced into r3. This reproduces rx35's
constraint with the actual a5 compiler. It is evidence of cross-block slot
reuse, not evidence that the source already matches retail.

Baseline versus final UWD capture proves identical no-opt weights and graph
colors. For v42/v43/v46/v47, graph degrees are 7/8/8/9, coloring priorities
15/14/11/10 and physical registers r0/r3/r0/r3 in both captures. The ADD
sources alone reverse in pre-color PCode. The final candidate has no register
forcing and no allocator-priority change disguised as a register fix.
